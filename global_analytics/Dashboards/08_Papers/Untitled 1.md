THE ENIGMA: The Minimum Observation Cost
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════

Landauer's Principle says erasing 1 bit costs at minimum:
E_min = k_B T ln(2)

At room temperature (300K), that's about 3×10⁻²¹ Joules.

But here's the puzzle:

**How much energy does it ACTUALLY take for a CONSCIOUS 
observer to collapse a wave function?**

We know it can't be less than Landauer's bound.
But is it exactly that? Or more?

🧮 THE CHALLENGE:
Can you design an experiment that measures the actual 
thermodynamic cost of conscious observation?

What we'd need:
  • Isolated quantum system
  • Conscious observer performing measurement
  • Sensitive calorimeter detecting heat release
  • Control for all other energy sources

💡 WHY IT MATTERS:
If conscious observation costs MORE than Landauer's bound,
that implies consciousness has additional thermodynamic 
overhead - which tells us something about its physical nature.

If it costs EXACTLY the bound, consciousness might be 
the most efficient possible information processor.
